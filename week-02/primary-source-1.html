📖 PRIMARY SOURCE 2.2
Theodore Roosevelt, Ranch Life and the Hunting-Trail (1888), excerpt
The free ranchman in his manner of life shows more kinship to an Arab sheik than to a sleek city merchant or tradesman... it is the life of men who live in the open, who tend their herds on horseback, who go armed and ready to guard their lives by their own prowess... Men who go into the business now-a-days, if they are already familiar with the actual work of cattle-raising, and are men of good sense, courage, and business capacity, can hardly fail to do well... [But] Eastern men who invest their money in cattle without knowing anything of the business, or who trust all to their subordinates, are naturally enough likely to incur heavy losses... The great free ranches, with their barbarous, picturesque, and curiously fascinating surroundings, mark a primitive stage of existence... and, like the similar stages which have gone before, [are] doomed to pass away.
Theodore Roosevelt, Ranch Life and the Hunting-Trail (New York: The Century Co., 1888), excerpted
