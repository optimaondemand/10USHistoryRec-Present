📖 PRIMARY SOURCE 13.4
Northern Securities Co. v. United States, 193 U.S. 197 (1904), Majority Opinion
Where there is a combination by several individuals separately owning stock in two competing railroad companies engaged in interstate commerce, to place the control of both in a single corporation... the resulting combination is a direct restraint of trade by destroying competition, and is illegal... So long as Congress keeps within the limits of its authority as defined by the Constitution... its regulations of interstate... commerce, whether founded in wisdom or not, must be submitted to by all.
Justice John Marshall Harlan, majority opinion, <i>Northern Securities Co. v. United States</i>, 193 U.S. 197 (1904), excerpted
